I recently got my first library card, and the first thing I did with it was create an account on Libby. If you’re not familiar, Libby is a great service for borrowing audiobooks and ebooks from your local library.

Like with physical media, your library has a limited number of “copies” of a book they can lend out through Libby, and so you’ll often times have to wait for a more popular title. It’s certainly an odd system; unlike physical books, digital books don’t take up shelf space, and aren’t limited by how many copies a library can reasonably have in their collection. However, I understand that they’re limited by the stupidity of copyright law, so I won’t hold it against Libby or the library system.

My first book, which I am currently listening to, is [Internet for the People by Ben Tarnoff](https://share.libbyapp.com/title/5537829). I’m only a couple chapters in, but it’s been a sobering look at how the internet got to the state that it’s in today.

One thing I did notice is that, Libby does support chapters, Internet for the People, and I assume other books as well, don’t make use of that support. It will be interesting to see how many books actually make use of chapter markers, and how many don’t as I listen to more.

I’ve really neglected reading books for quite a while now. Despite my many attempts over the years, I find it very difficult to _finish_ books. I have the best luck with audio books, but they’re expensive, and I’d rather support my local library than Audible/Amazon.
