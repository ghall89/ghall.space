It used to be that, while Apple’s software wasn’t always the best, it was worth it if you didn’t need anything fancy and just wanted something that played nice with the OS and “just worked”. Now, it seems Apple has been slowly "enshittifying" their apps to the point where I've been seriously considering alternatives for the first time.

One big example that affected my workflow is iWork. It was never the best office suite, but it was freem and did enough to be useful for people like me. But, as of their most recent major update as part of their Creator Studio subscription, they started inserting ads for said subscription.[^1] It feels tacky, like something Microsoft would do.

They've also started inserting ads into the Maps app. What is even the point of using Maps if it's going to have ads like every other map service?

More recently, there have [been hints](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/10/apple-considering-ads-inside-visual-intelligence/) that Apple is planning to implement ads in their visual intelligence features. Maybe the type of people who would use this feature don't care, but it leaves me wondering whats the advantage of using this over, say, a dedicated visual intelligence app that has ads and probably works better?

The Apple advantage was never that their apps were better than the compeition. It's that they were premium-feeling experiences for people for whom "good enough" is enough. No ads, no fluff. Once they start giving up on that advantage, then what's the point?

[^1]: The subscription itself is pretty ridiculous, as it includes Final Cut Pro, Motion, and Logic Pro. Those are far more niche than a word processor and spreadsheet, so it should really be a different subscription. But that's a whole other topic...
