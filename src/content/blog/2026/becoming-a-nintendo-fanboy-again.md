---
title: 'Becoming a Nintendo Fanboy Again'
pubDate: '7/5/2026'
tags: ['Gaming', 'Tech', 'Life']
---

I was a Nintendo-only gamer up until 2010 when I bought my first non-Nintendo console, the PlayStation 3. Since then, I basically always had both a PlayStation and Nintendo device in my home, and I was pretty happy with that arrangement.

But, with the rising costs of hardware, that future doesn't seem so attractive anymore. Sony's seeming determination to wreck the PlayStation brand over the last several years, combined with the mainstream AAA game industry losing the plot and producing increasingly bloated games, Nintendo seems like the only responsible choice for me going forward. 

I have no intention of ditching my PlayStation 5, which I picked up back in 2021 for what now looks like a bargain price of $500 USD, but I have no desire to pick up the inevitable PS6 when that comes out. I've been mostly left disappointed in terms of games targeting current gen hardware; notable exceptions being Baldur's Gate 3, Ghost of Yotei, Final Fantasy VII Rebirth, and Cyberpunk 2077[^1]. Every other new release I've played and enjoyed since 2021 and felt was worth my time was also available on PS4 - Horizon: Forbidden West comes to mind. That's paltry compared to previous generations.

At $500, it's borderline whether the PS5 was worth it for me – I'm leaning towards yes because the games that were worth it were excellent, and the performance improvements for PS4 and cross-gen games cannot be overstated. But looking at the trajectory of hardware prices, between the obscene memory prices, and the console subsidization model basically being killed off because of free-to-play titles like Fortnite, I'd be shocked if the PS6 costs less than $1000 USD.[^2]

Even if it doesn't cost that much, it will still likely cost more than a PS5. As such, it will be hard to justify upgrading when, in all likelihood, cross-generational games will become even _more_ common. Combine that with the fact that Sony has just completely lost touch with its audience by going all-in on live service titles (even [delaying a sequel to Horizon: Forbidden West in favor of a multi-player spin-off nobody asked for](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/horizon/horizon-3-is-probably-not-coming-for-a-long-time-as-most-of-the-studio-is-reportedly-working-on-multiplayer-spin-off-hunters-gathering-instead/)), and [killing physical games](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/07/01/physical-disc-production-ending-in-january-2028-for-new-games-releasing-on-playstation-consoles/)[^3], it feels incredibly ludicrous to support their next platform.

Many customers are signaling that they intend to move to PC, but for me that's not an option – I'm not going to pay $1500 (or much more, depending on memory prices) for a decent gaming PC. That leaves me with Nintendo, and, even at the current inflated price point, the Switch 2 seems like the only sensible platform to invest in at this point. 

Again, I have no intention of getting rid of my PlayStation 5 anytime soon simply because of my pre-existing library of games going back about a decade, but, when I eventually (hopefully??) have the disposable income, I see the Switch 2 being my last console purchase for this decade, if not beyond.

I'm becoming a Nintendo fanboy again. 

[^1]: Technically Cyberpunk 2077 was on PS4 but it was a mess, and never got the Phantom Liberty DLC.

[^2]: I'm basing this purely on the current price of the PS5 Pro, which is currently $900 USD.

[^3]: I don't object personally, I buy all my games on PlayStation digitally, but I do have an objection to removing the option for those who want it.
