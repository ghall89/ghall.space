This is a blog post that had been percolating in my brain for probably about a week or so, and one that I had thought that perhaps I shouldn't write. Internet discourse, especially around politics, isn't really the type of thing I feel qualified to write about, and I'm not sure what I could possibly add to the discussion. But I keep seeing a lot of bad takes, and I felt the need, as a straight, white, cisgender male, to speak up.

I am, to be clear, writing about Federico Viticci, John Vorhees, and MacStories, and the ill-advised return to Twitter[^1].

Here's the thing – whatever the reasoning was to not only bring MacStories back to Twitter, but also for Federico and John to start posting there again, doesn't matter. What matters is they did this in direct contradiction to their past stated values, and, from what I've seen from posts by other people involved with MacStories, without informing the rest of the team. To me, that clearly communicates that Federico and John _knew_ there would be fallout, but either didn't care or believed it didn't matter.

What's been so bizarre is seeing so many other high-profile members of the Apple community, many of whom have stopped posting on Twitter for what I'd say are good reasons, are either staying silent or defending the move as a "tech choice". It's ridiculous, cowardly, and basically calls into question how strongly they believe in their own convictions.

Something a lot of these takes miss is that people feel _lied to_. MacStories went all in on Mastodon when Twitter was bought by Elon Musk, and made a statement that they would be leaving Twitter because of the direction the site was going. Later they made it clear they wouldn't be supporting Meta's platforms because of Mark Zuckerberg's cozying up with the current US presidential administration.

I cannot speak for the LGBTQ+ community, African Americans, immigrants, or anyone else targeted by the bigotry espoused by Musk and his ilk. But I can speak for myself, someone who considers himself an ally, and strongly believes in equality and tolerance, and when Federico said he believed all these things too, and that MacStories wouldn't enable platforms that directly benefitted people who didn't believe in those things, I put my trust in that. Perhaps that was naive of me.

The other complaint I've seen from people defending, or indeed being purely indifferent, to the move has been a "lack of nuance" in the criticism of MacStories. With most things in life, I'd agree, things are generally not black-and-white. But I struggle to see the nuance in proclaiming to be an ally, but then turning around and setting your stated principles aside when it's inconvenient to have morals.

Finally, there's the argument that we can't criticize Federico and John because we support Apple and their products, and they've caused a lot of harm too. The whole topic of ethical consumption is far beyond the scope of this post, but to put it briefly; our responsibility as consumers is to do the best we can. Depending on the category, totally ethical consumption is possible, but in many area's its not. I choose Apple not because I believe it's 100% ethical, but because it's the least unethical option based on the information I have available. That doesn't preclude me from criticizing someone for abandoning their stated values to support a company that is objectively worse by every measure.

Anyway, I don't claim to have the perfect take on this, and I admit that I probably got some stuff wrong. I'm always open to learning how I can be a better ally, and a more well-rounded human.

[^1]: I refuse to call it X, and I am not going to keep writing Twitter/X for this whole post.
